Research

Research Focus

My research examines how humans interact with data-driven systems, and how we can and should use the data those interactions produce. I'm particularly interested in what happens to human connection as it becomes increasingly algorithmically mediated, and now, algorithmically produced: whether the systems we build can actually tell us what people are feeling, and what we owe people when they can't.

This throughline runs across a broader interest in human-computer interaction, data management practices, and mental health technologies. Earlier work examined how computing practitioners, from HCI researchers to civic and non-profit data workers, navigate the complexities of working with sensitive data. Current and future work turns that same question around, asking whether platforms' own behavioral data, engagement, trace data, and now AI-generated content, can be trusted to represent human experience at all.

Across all of this, I develop systems, frameworks, and empirical accounts that support ethical practices centered on data flourishing: human-centered approaches that prioritize dignity and well-being over mere compliance, moving from compliance-focused approaches toward care-centered, contextually-sensitive methods honoring individual dignity and collective responsibility.

Current Work

Operationalizing Diffracted Belonging

Ongoing

Recent HCI scholarship has identified diffracted belonging: the felt connection people experience toward strangers and creators reflected back at them across a personalized feed, each often representing a different facet of the self, without any direct interaction required. This experience has been richly described qualitatively, but never directly elicited on its own terms, nor tested against how people actually engage with the content that produces it. My current project addresses this directly, examining whether the engagement signals platforms already collect, likes, comments, shares, and simply viewing, actually correspond to this felt experience, rather than assuming they do.

  • Semi-structured interviews anchored in participants' own historical TikTok engagement data, rather than free recall or live scrolling
  • Posts sampled deliberately across a person's full range of engagement, passive, reactive, interactive, and contributory, deliberately including content viewed but never acted on
  • Synthesizes engagement typologies from communication research, consumer behavior, and platform-specific TikTok scholarship to sample engagement more precisely than any single existing framework
  • Applies an explicit coding definition distinguishing diffracted belonging from adjacent experiences, general enjoyment, parasocial attachment to a single creator, and novelty-driven curiosity
  • Designed to stand as a complete, standalone qualitative contribution while also producing groundwork for future measurement development

Mental Health Data Ethics and Practitioner Challenges

Through semi-structured interviews with eleven HCI researchers developing predictive and semi-automated mental health systems, our research team investigated the disconnect between ethical intentions and practical capabilities in mental health technology research.

  • Conducted thematic analysis revealing systematic challenges in mental health tech development
  • Identified gaps between diverse disciplinary training leading to inconsistent ethical practices
  • Documented IRB struggles to evaluate emerging technologies like large language models
  • Revealed fundamental ambiguity around what constitutes "health data" in computing research
  • Exposed significant gaps between published methods and actual research practices
  • Demonstrated how field fragmentation leaves practitioners without adequate support for vulnerable populations

Datum Fieldnotes: Supporting Civic and Non-Profit Data Work

In collaboration with Dr. Betsy DiSalvo and Dr. Annabel Rothschild, developed a Google Sheets add-on supporting data documentation in civic and non-profit (CNP) organizations. The tool addresses a critical tension: data work is mission-critical for demonstrating impact, yet diverts resources from direct service.

  • Conducted user testing with twelve CNP data workers to understand organizational needs
  • Developed tool that automates micro-documentation and enables rich contextualization
  • Created comprehensive curriculum to support data infrastructure literacy across skill levels
  • Addressed challenges of lean budgets, high turnover, and minimal training capacity
  • Recognized that CNP datasets frequently become training data for ML systems in social services
  • Published findings at CHI 2025 Late Breaking Work
